# TranscribeVid: a zero-rupee marketing and brand plan

For you, the owner. Cost: 0 rupees. What it takes is time: the plan assumes **about one hour a day**. If you have
less, do the first item of each week and skip the rest; the order is by effect.

Facts this plan is built on (checked 28 October 2026):

- The site is two days old, so it has no links from other sites and no reputation yet. This is the real reason it does
  not rank. Everything below is about earning that reputation without paying for it.
- What is different about it, and true: free, no sign-up, no cap on how many videos, and the video is not uploaded: the
  work happens on the visitor's own device. Fifteen other languages besides English. Few tools in the lists I read say
  all of these together (SubtitleKit, for one, also works in the browser with no account, so never say "the only one").
- Money comes from ads on text pages (AdSense), so every real visitor is worth something and nothing has to be sold.
- Already built and free: a logo and icons, a share image (`og-image.png`), an About page that names the maker, 58 English
  pages and 510 translated ones, listings text ready to paste (`deploy/LISTINGS.md`), a day-by-day Google plan
  (`deploy/INDEXING-PLAN.md`).

I cannot promise a number of visitors. A new site usually gets its first search clicks 2 to 6 weeks after its pages are
indexed, and how fast it grows depends mostly on how many real sites link to it. The plan below is how to get those links
honestly.

---

## 1. The brand in one page

**Promise (say it the same way everywhere):**
> Free video to text. No sign-up. No cap. Your video never leaves your device.

**Who it is for, in the order they are likely to find you:**

| Who | What they need | How to speak to them |
|---|---|---|
| Students, researchers | Lectures and interviews turned into text | "Transcribe a one-hour lecture for free" |
| YouTubers, reel makers | Subtitles (SRT, VTT) for their own videos | "Get subtitles for your video in minutes" |
| Journalists, podcasters | Interviews and episodes as text, with times | "Timestamps on every line" |
| Teams and small businesses | Meeting recordings as notes | "No account, no monthly minutes" |
| People who care where their files go | A tool that does not upload | "It runs in your browser, check the network tab yourself" |
| Hindi, Urdu, Bengali, Arabic, Thai, Vietnamese and other speakers | A tool in their own language | Post in their language, with the page in their language |

**Voice:** plain, honest, a little modest. Say what it does not do (no speaker names, no links, a phone is slow). That
is your edge: the big tools hide their limits, you print them. Never say "best", "fastest", "100 % accurate".

**Proof you can use** (all true today): a dated table of 13 tools' free limits, each quoted from its own page
(`/guides/free-video-to-text-tools-compared`); the network-panel test that shows nothing is uploaded; the maker's name on the
About page.

**One name, everywhere.** Pick one handle and use it on every platform: `transcribevid` (check it is free on YouTube,
Instagram, X, GitHub, Reddit, LinkedIn, Product Hunt; if not, add the same suffix everywhere). One logo (`favicon.svg`),
one colour, one tagline, one e-mail from the About page. Consistent names are how search engines and AI assistants
learn that all those pages are the same thing.

**Free tools for all of this:** Canva (free) for images, CapCut or OBS Studio (free) for screen recordings, a free
Google Sheet to track everything, Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools and Cloudflare Web Analytics (all free, already set up).

## 2. The channels, ranked by effect for zero rupees

### A. Get listed (week 1, about 4 hours in total)
Copy-paste text is ready in `deploy/LISTINGS.md`. In this order: AlternativeTo, SaaSHub, Product Hunt (profile now,
launch in week 4), GitHub (a README that links the site), Indie Hackers, dev.to. Each listing is a permanent link and
a place where people comparing tools look. Add TranscribeVid as an alternative on the pages of Otter, TurboScribe,
Notta, Rev, Descript, Sonix and Happy Scribe.

### B. Be the useful answer (a little every day, 20 minutes)
People ask "how do I transcribe a video" every day on Reddit, Quora, Stack Exchange and YouTube comments, and in
Hindi and other languages on Facebook groups, Telegram groups and Quora Hindi. Search the question, then:
1. Read the community's rules first. Some ban links; then answer without one.
2. Answer the question fully, so the answer is good even without your site.
3. At the end, say you made the tool, once, with the link to the page that fits (the how-to guide, the MP4 page, the
   WhatsApp voice note page).
4. Never post the same text twice. Never reply to your own post from another account.

Five good answers a week beat fifty spammy ones; spam gets the site banned from the place where it matters.

### C. Short videos that show the tool working (one a week, 2 hours)
A 30 to 60 second screen recording: drop a file, the text appears, download the SRT. No face needed. Post the same
video on YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, X and LinkedIn, with the page address in the description. Ten first topics:
1. Transcribe a one-hour lecture for free, no sign-up
2. Subtitles (SRT) for your reel in 3 minutes
3. WhatsApp voice note to text
4. Hindi video to text (record it in Hindi)
5. "I checked 13 free transcription tools: here are their real limits"
6. How to check that a website really does not upload your file (the network panel)
7. Turn a meeting recording into notes
8. MP4 to text on a phone: what works and what is slow
9. Transcribe an interview and fix the names fast
10. Urdu, Bengali and Arabic video to text

Make the same video in other languages for the languages you have pages in: the pages are already there, the
competition is thin, and these audiences are large.

### D. Ask for links, politely (two messages a day, 20 minutes)
This is the one thing that moves search rankings most. The messages are in `deploy/LISTINGS.md` (sections 10 and 10b).
Who to write to:
- Authors of "best free transcription / video to text tools" articles: offer the sourced comparison table.
- Accessibility and e-learning offices of universities and colleges that keep a page of "free captioning tools":
  suggest it as a no-sign-up option for staff and students.
- Journalism and research-skills pages ("tools for reporters", "digital research toolkit").
- Whisper and AI-tool lists on GitHub ("awesome" lists): open a pull request that adds one line.
- Newsletters and blogs about free tools or productivity: send one short message, no attachment.
One message per person, no follow-up pestering. Keep a sheet: who, date, answer, link live or not.

### E. Launch moments (once each; spaced a few weeks apart)
1. **Show HN** (week 3) and **Product Hunt** (week 4): the texts are in `LISTINGS.md`. Pick a weekday, stay online all
   day, answer every comment. A launch is worth about a day of attention and a few permanent links, not a stream of
   visitors; treat it as a link source.
2. **Reddit** (week 5): one post in one fitting community, as the maker, with the honest limits. Only where self-posts
   are allowed.
3. **Your own story** (week 6): a short article "I built a transcription site that never uploads your video" on dev.to,
   Hashnode or your own page, with real numbers and what went wrong. These get shared and cited more than ads do.

### F. Keep the site itself earning its place (one page a week, 2 hours)
- One new page or guide a week from the list in `docs/in-browser/PROGRESS.md` (mp4-to-srt, run Whisper locally,
  transcribe audio to text, Hindi-first pages). Each new page is another way to be found.
- Refresh the comparison table once a quarter with the date, or it turns into a liability.
- Translate each new page into the 15 languages once its English version is indexed.

### G. Things that cost nothing and are easy to forget
- Ask every friend, student and colleague who uses it to bookmark it. A visitor who comes back by name is worth more
  than a click from search.
- Put the address in your e-mail signature, your WhatsApp and LinkedIn profiles, and your Instagram bio.
- Reply to every comment, e-mail and bug report within a day. The first 50 users are your testers and your reviewers.
- Later, when real users have said kind things: ask permission, then quote them with their name. Never write one yourself.

## 3. A 12-week calendar (about an hour a day)

| Week | Focus | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Listings (A); handles and bios; Google Day 1 to 3 requests | 5 listings live, one handle everywhere |
| 2 | Google Day 4 to 6 requests; first two videos (C); five useful answers (B) | 2 videos posted, 5 answers |
| 3 | Show HN; 10 link requests (D); Bing: send the sitemap screenshot | Post made, 10 messages sent |
| 4 | Product Hunt launch; 10 more link requests; one new page (F) | Launch done, sheet kept |
| 5 | Reddit post; the Hindi video; Hindi, Urdu, Bengali community posts | Posts in 3 languages |
| 6 | Your story article (E3); translate the new page; check Search Console | Article live |
| 7 to 8 | Keep the weekly rhythm: 1 video, 5 answers, 10 messages, 1 page. Add GitHub "awesome" list pull requests | The same rhythm for two weeks |
| 9 | Review: which channel sent visitors? Do more of that, drop the rest | One page of notes |
| 10 to 12 | Same rhythm; refresh the comparison table; ask users who wrote nice things for a quote | Quarter review |

**Weekly review (20 minutes, Sunday):** Search Console (impressions, clicks, which searches), Bing Webmaster, Cloudflare
Web Analytics (where visitors come from), AdSense. Write three numbers in your sheet: impressions, clicks, new links.
If a channel brought visitors, do more of it; if two weeks bring nothing, drop it.

**Targets, not promises:** by the end of month 1, most English pages indexed and ten listings live; by month 3, steady
daily search clicks and links from at least ten real sites. How many clicks a day that gives depends on the links.

## 4. Rules that protect the income

1. **Only real visitors.** Never buy traffic, never use click exchanges or "traffic bots", never click your own ads and
   never ask anyone to. AdSense closes accounts for invalid traffic, and the whole plan runs on that account.
2. **No fake reviews, ratings, accounts or comments.** They get found and they end the brand.
3. **No claims you cannot back.** Say "free, no sign-up, nothing uploaded", not "best" or "most accurate". No legal,
   medical or compliance promises ("HIPAA-safe" and the like): say how it works and let people decide.
4. **Say you are the maker** in every community post. Hidden self-promotion is punished everywhere.
5. **Keep the promise in the product:** no watermark, no sign-up wall, no "free trial". The whole brand is that the
   word "free" is true.

## 5. Ideas to decide later (not needed now)

- A small "Transcribed with TranscribeVid" line at the foot of an exported PDF, **off by default**, as an option for
  people who want to share it. It would turn every shared transcript into a link, but it must never be added silently.
- A "Copy transcript" button next to the download buttons (keeps the file on the device).
- A public GitHub repository with the code, if you are willing to share it: developers link to code more than to anything else.
- A YouTube channel of tutorials in Hindi: very little competition, and the viewers match the tool.
